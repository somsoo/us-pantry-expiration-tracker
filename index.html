    📅 What the dates mean

    • Best if used by: a quality date, not a safety date. USDA says that except for infant formula, date labels are not safety dates and are not required by federal law.
    • Sell by: tells the store how long to display the product for sale. Buy before it, then go by storage times at home.
    • Use by: if a product has a use-by date, USDA advises following that date. Never use infant formula after its use-by date.
    • After the date: refrigerated food should still be safe if it was handled properly and kept at 40°F or below for the recommended storage time. If you have any doubt, throw it out.

    ❓ Frequently asked questions

    Is food still safe after the best-by date?

    Usually, if it was stored properly. USDA says that, except for infant formula, date labels are not safety dates; they show when food is at its best quality. After the date passes, refrigerated food should still be safe if it was handled properly and kept at 40°F or below for the recommended storage time. If you have any doubt about a food, throw it out.

    What is the difference between sell-by and use-by?

    A sell-by date tells the store how long to display the product for sale. If a product has a use-by date, USDA advises following that date. If it has only a sell-by date or no date at all, cook or freeze fresh meat and poultry within the recommended storage times.

    How long do leftovers last in the fridge?

    Three to four days, according to FoodSafety.gov. If you will not eat them in time, freeze them; frozen leftovers keep their best quality for 2 to 6 months. Refrigerate cooked food within two hours, or within one hour if it is above 90°F.

    Why does infant formula have a date?

    Unlike other foods, infant formula must be dated under FDA rules. USDA advises not to feed a baby formula after its use-by date, because it may no longer have enough nutrients.
